Step 2 • Strategy Spotter

Identify Adler's Rhetorical Choices

Read each excerpt below. Before tapping, jot down in your notebook: What specific move is Adler making here, and why? Then tap to check your analytical thinking.

Excerpt 1 Tap to reveal strategy
"The adverse impacts of plastic bags are undeniable... plastic grocery bags were the seventh most common item collected during the Ocean Conservancy’s cleanup... NYC collects 1,700 tons of single-use carry-out bags every week..."

Rhetorical Choice: Strategic Concession & Hard Data

Adler acknowledges the real harms of plastic bags using civic statistics. This builds credibility (ethos) with eco-conscious readers by showing he is not a plastic apologist before he introduces his counter-argument.

Excerpt 2 Tap to reveal strategy
"‘People look at [paper] and say it’s degradable, therefore it’s much better... but it’s not in terms of climate change impact,’ says David Tyler, a professor of chemistry at the University of Oregon..."

Rhetorical Choice: Expert Scientific Testimony

Adler incorporates a chemistry professor to dismantle a common misconception. Using expert authority reframes a moral debate into an objective scientific question about carbon mass and life-cycle energy.

Excerpt 3 Tap to reveal strategy
"Only 2.4 percent of the world’s cropland is planted with cotton, yet it accounts for 24 percent of the global market for insecticides... A pound of cotton requires more than 5,000 gallons of water..."

Rhetorical Choice: Comparative Resource Analysis

Adler uses shocking agricultural statistics regarding cotton tote production to challenge the assumption that reusable alternatives are inherently eco-friendly, forcing readers to examine trade-offs.

Excerpt 4 Tap to reveal strategy
"‘Eat one less meat dish a week—that’s what will have a real impact on the environment,’ says Tyler. ‘It’s what we put in the bag at the grocery store that really matters.’"

Rhetorical Choice: Shift in Perspective (Reframing the Problem)

Adler concludes by broadening the scope from bag policy to broader dietary overconsumption, leaving the reader with a practical, high-impact action step rather than a superficial policy fix.

Step 3 • The Formula

Introduction Architecture

Sentence 1 (Context): In [Year/Publication], [Author] addresses [Occasion/Audience] regarding [Subject].

Sentence 2 (Purpose): By [Action/Reframing], [Author] seeks to [Core Purpose].

Sentences 3–4 (Thesis): [Author] accomplishes this by [Specific Choice A] and [Specific Choice B], ultimately moving the audience to [Target Goal].

🚨 AP Thesis Guardrail

Do NOT list generic rhetorical devices (e.g., "Adler uses ethos, logos, and pathos"). Instead, use the choices you unlocked in Step 2 (e.g., "incorporating scientific expert testimony" or "analyzing comparative resource-intensity data").
